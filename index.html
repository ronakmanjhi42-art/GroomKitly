What is a pet grooming profit calculator?

A pet grooming profit calculator shows how much money one grooming appointment actually earns after you subtract the costs of doing the job. Many groomers know what they charge, but not what is left once labor, shampoo, blades, fuel, and card fees are paid. This tool does that math for you, as you type.

Revenue, expenses, and profit

  • Revenue is money coming in: the service price plus any tip.
  • Expenses are money going out because of that appointment.
  • Profit is what remains: revenue minus expenses.

Why labor and travel costs matter

If you groom alone, it is easy to treat your own time as free. Putting a labor rate on every hour shows whether a long, difficult groom pays you fairly. Mobile groomers should also count fuel, vehicle wear, and drive time, because a cheap-looking job across town can cost more than it earns.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the service price and any tip for a typical appointment.
  2. Enter how many hours the groom takes, start to finish, and the hourly labor cost you want to pay yourself or an employee.
  3. Add supplies, travel, overhead (such as a share of rent, insurance, or software), and other costs.
  4. Add your card processing percentage, if you take card payments.
  5. Read the results. They update as you type. Try a higher price to see how profit changes.

How to calculate profit per grooming appointment

Add up every cost tied to the appointment, then subtract that total from the money you collect. Dividing the profit by the hours worked gives profit per hour, which is the fairest way to compare a quick bath with a full haircut.

How to estimate a break-even grooming price

The break-even price is the lowest service price at which profit is exactly $0. The calculator holds your tip fixed and applies the card fee to both the price and the tip:

Break-Even Price = [Non-Processing Expenses ÷ (1 − Fee Rate)] − Tip

Fee Rate is the processing percentage divided by 100. Non-processing expenses are labor, supplies, travel, overhead, and other costs. If the result is below zero, the calculator shows $0.00. If the fee is 100% or more, no price can cover the costs, so it shows "Not achievable". Break-even is a floor, not a target: you still need room for profit.

Common grooming pricing mistakes

  • Charging a flat price for dogs that take very different amounts of time.
  • Leaving out your own labor, or paying yourself nothing.
  • Ignoring travel time and fuel on mobile routes.
  • Forgetting card fees, insurance, software, and rent.
  • Counting tips as guaranteed income when setting prices.
  • Never revisiting prices as supply and fuel costs change.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good profit margin for dog grooming?

It varies with your location, services, and how you pay yourself. Instead of chasing one number, decide what hourly profit and margin you need to cover your goals, then use the calculator to test prices against it.

Should I include my own time as labor cost?

Yes, in most cases. Using a realistic hourly rate for yourself shows whether the business pays you fairly. If you want to see profit before paying yourself, enter $0 as the labor rate.

What counts as overhead?

Overhead is the cost of running the business that is not tied to one dog: rent, insurance, software, licenses, vehicle payments, and marketing. Estimate a fair share per appointment by dividing monthly overhead by your typical number of monthly appointments.

How do I use this for a mobile grooming business?

Enter fuel and vehicle costs in the travel field, include drive time in the duration if you want to be paid for it, and add van-related costs such as water, generator, or maintenance under overhead or other expenses.

Why does break-even show "Not achievable"?

This appears when the processing fee is 100% or higher. At that rate the fee takes all of the money collected, so no price can cover your costs.

Is my data saved or shared?

No. Calculations happen in your browser, and the page does not store or send your numbers.

Is this tax or accounting advice?

No. The calculator gives estimates from the numbers you enter. For tax or financial decisions, talk to a qualified accountant.
